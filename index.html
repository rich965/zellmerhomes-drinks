In Season

Watermelon Margarita★

2 oz LALO Blanco · 1 oz Cointreau · 2 oz watermelon puree · ¾ oz fresh lime · ½ oz honey syrup

Watermelon is mostly water, so it thins a drink more than it flavours one. The ounce of Cointreau is what gives it back its shape — orange and structure where the melon brings only sweetness. That puts it on the Classic Margarita backbone rather than Tommy's, with the puree riding on top.

Shake hard, fine-strain over fresh ice — a puree turns gritty otherwise. Pinch of salt in the shaker as well as half the rim: a half rim means half the sips get salt and half do not. Lime wheel.

Watermelon Spritz★

2 oz watermelon puree · 1 oz St-Germain · ½ oz fresh lime · 3 oz prosecco · Club soda to finish

No base spirit at all, about 8% ABV, and the prosecco does the lifting. The lime is what keeps the melon from reading as candy.

Shake the puree, St-Germain and lime, fine-strain into a wine glass of ice, then prosecco and a splash of soda. One gentle stir. Mint sprig and a watermelon wedge.

Air Mail★

1½ oz Flor de Caña 12 · ½ oz fresh lime · ¾ oz honey syrup · 1½ oz prosecco

A rum-based French 75, and the honey is the whole point — there is nothing else sweet in the glass, so the syrup has nowhere to hide. Older than it looks: it turns up in a Bacardi pamphlet in the 1930s.

Shake the first three hard, strain into a highball of ice, top with prosecco. One brief stir. Lime twist, or a mint tip.

Air Mail No. 2★

1½ oz Flor de Caña 12 · ½ oz Cointreau · ½ oz fresh lime · ¼ oz honey syrup · 1½ oz prosecco

Orange liqueur carries half the sweetening here, so the honey drops to a quarter ounce — rounder, a little stronger, and the orange picks up the same note the rum already takes as a garnish. Meant to be poured beside the original, not instead of it.

Shake the first four, strain into a highball of ice, top with prosecco. One brief stir. Orange twist, not lime.

Tommy's Margarita

2 oz LALO Blanco · 1 oz fresh lime · ½ oz honey syrup

No orange liqueur. Honey stands in for agave nectar and is arguably better.

Shake hard, strain over fresh ice.

Classic Margarita

2 oz LALO Blanco · ¾ oz Cointreau · 1 oz fresh lime

Cointreau is what the original is built on — orange with structure rather than orange with sugar, and at this ratio it is most of what you taste.

Shake, strain over fresh ice. Salt half the rim. Mr. Boston still does the job if you are making a round of six.

Smoked Chocolate Añejo★

2 oz Herradura Añejo · ¼ oz honey syrup · 3 dashes Spiced Chocolate bitters · 1 dash Angostura

Chocolate bitters and aged agave sounds like a gimmick and isn't. With apple smoke it's the best drink in this book.

Smoke the empty glass 20 seconds with apple or cherry. Big cube, build, stir 30 seconds. Orange peel.

Oaxacan Margarita

1½ oz LALO Blanco · ½ oz mezcal · 1 oz fresh lime · ½ oz honey syrup

Split base — the LALO carries the body, the mezcal carries the smoke. A real bartender's move, not a compromise, and it stretches the mezcal bottle.

Shake hard, strain over fresh ice. Salt half the rim.

Smoky Rosa Paloma★

1 oz mezcal · ½ oz LALO Blanco · 1½ oz Sarti Rosa · ¾ oz fresh lime · Elderflower tonic to top

Jen's drink with smoke underneath it. Sarti's peach and floral against mezcal is a genuinely striking combination — make the original first, then this one, back to back.

Naked & Famous

¾ oz mezcal · ¾ oz Aperol · ¾ oz St-Germain · ¾ oz fresh lime

The modern classic is equal parts mezcal, Aperol, yellow Chartreuse and lime. There is no Chartreuse on the shelf, so St-Germain stands in — less herbal, more floral, and still very good.

Shake hard, strain up. No garnish needed.

Beer & Aperitivo

Tan Limes Aperol Shandy★

1½ oz Aperol · ¼ oz fresh lime · 8 oz Cape May Tan Limes

The lime is already in the beer, so you're stacking with it instead of fighting it. Better than the Corona version for exactly that reason.

Aperol and lime in the glass first, beer poured down the side so it doesn't foam over. Salt half the rim, lime wedge. No ice.

Corona Aperol Shandy

1½ oz Aperol · ½ oz fresh lime (lime, not lemon — Corona wants lime) · 8 oz Corona

Same method. Keep the Aperol at 1½ oz — the standard 2 oz bulldozes a beer this delicate.

Sarti Shandy★

2 oz Sarti Rosa · ¼ oz fresh lime · 8 oz Tan Limes or Corona

Jen's version. Lighter, pinker, fruitier, much less bitter — and it works with either beer.

Same method. Lemon twist.

Aperol Radler

Lower proof, sweeter, a daytime drink. Uses the lemonade.

Over ice in a tall glass. Lemon wheel.

House Originals

Barnegat Smoke★

1½ oz Rey Campero mezcal · ¾ oz cream of coconut · ½ oz pineapple juice · ¾ oz fresh lime · 2 dashes Spiced Chocolate bitters

Smoke and coconut fat is a real pairing and an underused one. The fat rounds mezcal's edge without covering it; the lime stops it becoming dessert.

Shake hard 15 seconds. Double-strain: through a fine mesh into a rocks glass over crushed ice — coconut cream leaves grit otherwise. Grate nutmeg. Lime wheel. Don't use the smoker; the mezcal already did.
If it reads sour  Add a pinch of salt — salt suppresses sourness directly and usually fixes it alone.
If that isn't enough  lime to ½ oz, and split the base ¾ oz mezcal / ¾ oz LALO to halve the lactic funk.
